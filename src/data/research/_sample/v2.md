This is a fictional note used to preview the research section in development. It compares three made-up ways to keep a sourdough starter alive while travelling, and how well each one holds up after a week away.

## Main finding

Drying the starter is still the most reliable method for trips longer than a week, and the new survey data shows it recovers faster than earlier reports suggested. Refrigeration works for short trips but the starter needs two or three feedings to recover. Freezing is the least predictable of the three, with about one in five frozen starters failing to revive.

## Methods compared

| Method | Longest tested absence | Feedings to recover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refrigerate | 10 days | 2 to 3 |
| Dry on parchment | 6 months | 2 to 3 |
| Freeze | 3 months | 4 to 6 |

## What to watch for

- A layer of grey liquid on top means the starter is hungry, not dead.
- A pink or orange tint means it has spoiled and should be thrown away.
- A sharp acetone smell usually fades after one feeding.
